# AR77-013｜METHOD RUNTIME

## Evidence Status

### KNOWN
Authoritative institutional evidence supports these recurring facts and statements:

- In his 1989 Pritzker acceptance speech, Gehry described architecture as having to solve complex problems, use technology, be safe and dry, respect context and neighbors, address social responsibility and satisfy the client, before confronting composition, scale, material and other artistic questions.
- The Pritzker jury recognized experimentation, collage-like juxtapositions of spaces and materials, and openness to risk rather than a single classifiable style.
- Gehry has repeatedly used many physical models as a design method. Fondation Louis Vuitton documents models in wood, plastic and metal used to examine site relationship, interior space and volume, with a confirmation model bridging physical and digital models.
- MoMA records extensive model-making in Gehry's work and Gehry himself has described models as his way of designing and communicating a whole project in three dimensions.
- Guggenheim Bilbao and Fondation Louis Vuitton document aerospace-derived digital tools used to translate mathematically/physically complex designs into coordinated construction.
- Walt Disney Concert Hall documents close collaboration among architect, acoustician and musicians, with audience–musician relationships and acoustic performance shaping the auditorium.
- MIT documents the Stata Center's goals around community, accidental encounters, flexible/interconnected workspaces, natural light and differentiated neighborhoods, while Gehry explicitly described balancing an intuitive approach with deliberate sense of place.

### SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION
For contemporary project runtime, these recurring practices can be translated into:
1. put people/program relationships and context before iconic image;
2. use iterative models as arguments, not presentation trophies;
3. compose differentiated spatial/massing parts where program/context benefits from difference;
4. test emotional/experiential intensity against ordinary architectural duties;
5. use available materials/craft directly, without fixing a signature palette;
6. integrate specialist performance early when it is causal;
7. use digital tools to translate and coordinate complex intent, not to generate complexity for its own sake;
8. keep composition revisable when use, performance or construction evidence contradicts it.

## Method Sequence

`PEOPLE + PROGRAM + CONTEXT`
→ `PRIMARY CONFLICT`
→ `PRIMARY ROUTE`
→ `3D MODEL / SPATIAL TEST`
→ `DIFFERENTIATED PARTS + RELATIONSHIPS`
→ `MOVEMENT / ENCOUNTER / EXPERIENCE`
→ `SPECIALIST + TECHNICAL TRANSLATION`
→ `PERFORMANCE + BUILDABILITY`
→ `REVISION`

## Causal vs Appearance

Transferable:
- relationship-first programming;
- model-led iteration;
- differentiated parts;
- collage/juxtaposition as a coordination question;
- movement and encounter;
- context dialogue;
- direct engagement with available craft/material;
- specialist collaboration;
- digital translation/coordination;
- willingness to revise.

Not default-transferable:
- titanium or stainless-steel skin;
- billowing curves;
- glass sails;
- fish motifs;
- leaning towers;
- fragmented suburban house imagery;
- exposed chain link/corrugated metal;
- any fixed “deconstructivist” geometry.

## Evidence Boundary

Gehry did not use the AR77 Route names, DNA names, 3–5 selection rule or A/B/C schema. These are operational distillation devices, not historical terminology.
