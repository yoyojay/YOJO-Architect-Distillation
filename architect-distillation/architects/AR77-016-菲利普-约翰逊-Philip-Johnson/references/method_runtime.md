# AR77-016｜METHOD RUNTIME

## Evidence Status

### KNOWN

- Philip Johnson was the founding director of MoMA's Department of Architecture and, with Henry-Russell Hitchcock, organized the 1932 exhibition that helped define and disseminate the International Style in the United States.
- Johnson's own architecture did not remain within that position. The Pritzker jury explicitly described his work as spanning decades and changing with new ideas, while his 1979 acceptance speech acknowledged shifting allegiances and the importance of history.
- The Glass House is documented by the National Trust as part of a site-wide composition of architecture, art and landscape; the transparent house and the contrasting Brick House form a deliberately related pair rather than a universal glass-box formula.
- MoMA's collection and exhibition history documents Johnson as curator, tastemaker and architect, demonstrating that selection, framing and architectural discourse were unusually important parts of his practice.
- Johnson's later work openly reintroduced historical reference and ornament, making it impossible to treat his career as one stable formal system.

### SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION

For contemporary project runtime, the useful recurring judgement is not one style but a set of operations:
1. define the project's architectural/cultural position;
2. read precedent critically and reframe it;
3. establish a strong primary order;
4. use framing, threshold, contrast and procession to make relationships legible;
5. test an architectural object against its site and counterpart conditions;
6. allow contradiction/reference only when it has a present project reason;
7. revise an inherited position when new evidence or cultural conditions demand it;
8. separate durable decision logic from historically dated imagery.

## Method Sequence

`PROJECT ROLE + CONTEXT`
→ `POSITION`
→ `PRECEDENT / COUNTERPOINT`
→ `PRIMARY CONFLICT`
→ `PRIMARY ROUTE`
→ `GEOMETRIC / SPATIAL ORDER`
→ `FRAME + THRESHOLD + SEQUENCE`
→ `PLAN / SECTION / MOVEMENT`
→ `CONTEMPORARY TEST`
→ `REVISION`

## Causal vs Appearance

Transferable:
- architectural positioning;
- critical use of precedent;
- strong primary geometric order;
- object/site counterpoint;
- framing and threshold;
- controlled sequence;
- contrast as a clarifying relation;
- willingness to revise architectural position.

Not default-transferable:
- transparent glass box;
- black steel grid;
- brick cylinder;
- classical/broken pediment;
- Chippendale crown;
- crystal geometry;
- historic ornament;
- any period-specific Johnson material/form package.

## Evidence Boundary

Johnson did not use the AR77 Route names, DNA names, 3–5 selection rule or A/B/C schema. These are operational distillation devices, not historical terminology.

Because his career changed significantly, any claim of a single permanent “Johnson method” must be treated cautiously. This runtime preserves only mechanisms supported across his curatorial/architectural practice or clearly bounded historical phases.
