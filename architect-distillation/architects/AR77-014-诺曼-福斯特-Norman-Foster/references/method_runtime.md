# AR77-014｜METHOD RUNTIME

## Evidence Status

### KNOWN
Authoritative evidence supports these recurring positions:

- Norman Foster's Pritzker biography records an early commitment to designing with economy of means and to producing efficient, environmentally sensitive structures.
- Foster + Partners describes its practice as an integrated studio combining architecture with engineering, structural/environmental engineering, industrial design, research, materials and other specialist disciplines.
- Foster + Partners states that research is integrated into projects and includes materials, energy/environment, systems/workflow, digital design/fabrication and performance feedback.
- Foster's Pritzker acceptance speech frames design as an optimistic process of integration and describes infrastructure, energy and resource questions as architectural concerns.
- The Norman Foster Foundation's work and education programs emphasize interdisciplinary thinking linking architecture, engineering, technology, infrastructure, cities and sustainability.
- Foster's documented projects repeatedly use environmental systems, structural logic, daylight, circulation and shared space as active components of architectural organization; these mechanisms vary by project rather than forming one fixed formal vocabulary.

### SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION
For contemporary project runtime, these recurring judgements can be translated into:
1. establish long-term purpose and measurable performance before image;
2. integrate architecture, engineering and environmental systems early;
3. use research/testing to challenge assumptions;
4. pursue economy of means without confusing lightness with fragility;
5. design for change when long life is expected;
6. let environmental systems generate space only when climate evidence supports them;
7. turn circulation/infrastructure into legible connection where useful;
8. judge systems across construction, operation, maintenance and future adaptation.

- research-led testing;
- economy of means;
- structural/material efficiency;
- environmental mediation;
- daylight/shared-space integration;
- legible circulation;
- long-life adaptability;
- infrastructure/public-realm thinking;
- lifecycle evaluation.

Not default-transferable:
- exposed steel;
- glass curtain wall;
- diagrid;
- triangulated roof;
- glass dome;
- giant atrium;
- service towers/masts;
- aerodynamic geometry;
- circular campus;
- any fixed “high-tech” palette.
